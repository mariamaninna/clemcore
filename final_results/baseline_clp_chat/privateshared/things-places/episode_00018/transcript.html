QUESTIONER: What is in the northwest?

The **northwest** is a region in the United States known for its rugged natural beauty, temperate rainforests, and vibrant cultural history. It includes the following states and areas:

### **Core States of the U.S. Northwest:**
- **Washington**
- **Oregon**
- **Idaho** (especially the northern and western parts)
- **Montana** (western and northern regions)
- **Wyoming** (western part, including the Bighorn Mountains)
- **Alaska** (often included in broader definitions, especially in cultural or geographic contexts)
- **British Columbia, Canada** (sometimes considered part of the broader "Northwest" in cross-border discussions)

---

### **Key Features of the Northwest:**

#### 1. **Geography & Landscapes**
- **Pacific Coast**: Long stretches of rugged coastline, including the Olympic Peninsula and the Oregon coast.
- **Mountains**: The Cascade Range (home to Mount Rainier, Mount St. Helens, and Mount Hood), the Sierra Nevada (northern edge), and the Rocky Mountains.
- **Forests**: Dense temperate rainforests (e.g., in the Olympic National Park and the Pacific Northwest), with towering Douglas firs, redwoods, and moss-covered trees.
- **Rivers & Waterfalls**: The Columbia River, Willamette River, and numerous waterfalls (e.g., Multnomah Falls).
- **Lakes & Glaciers**: Crater Lake (Oregon), Lake Chelan (Washington), and glaciers in the North Cascades.

#### 2. **Climate**
- **Maritime climate**: Cool, wet winters and mild, dry summers.
- **High rainfall** in western Washington and Oregon — especially in the Olympic Mountains, which receive some of the highest rainfall in the U.S.
- **Drier interior** in eastern Washington and Idaho (e.g., the Palouse region).

#### 3. **Culture & History**
- **Indigenous Peoples**: Home to many Native American tribes, including the Coast Salish, Nez Perce, Yakama, and Tlingit.
- **Frontier & Logging History**: Strong heritage from pioneers, fur traders, and the logging industry.
- **Environmental Movement**: A leader in conservation, sustainability, and eco-conscious living.
- **Tech & Innovation**: Seattle is a global hub for technology (Amazon, Microsoft, Boeing) and a center for the "green" economy.

#### 4. **Major Cities**
- **Seattle, Washington** – Tech, music (Nirvana, Pearl Jam), coffee culture, and the Space Needle.
- **Portland, Oregon** – Known for its arts, food scene, sustainability, and craft beer.
- **Spokane, Washington** – A hub for education and outdoor recreation.
- **Boise, Idaho** – Growing tech scene and outdoor access.
- **Missoula, Montana** – A college town with a strong arts and outdoor culture.

#### 5. **Natural Wonders**
- **Olympic National Park** (WA) – Rainforests, alpine zones, and coastline.
- **Mount Rainier National Park** (WA) – A massive stratovolcano and glacier-covered peak.
- **Columbia River Gorge** (OR/WA) – A dramatic canyon with waterfalls and hiking trails.
- **Yellowstone National Park** (partially in the northwest, mostly in Wyoming) – Geysers, wildlife, and geothermal activity.

#### 6. **Food & Drink**
- **Seafood**: Salmon, Dungeness crab, oysters, and clams.
- **Craft Beer**: Portland and Seattle are famous for their breweries.
- **Coffee Culture**: Seattle is the birthplace of Starbucks.
- **Local Produce**: Apples, cherries, and berries from the Pacific Northwest.

- **Northwest Asia**: Includes countries like Turkey, Iran, and the Caucasus.
