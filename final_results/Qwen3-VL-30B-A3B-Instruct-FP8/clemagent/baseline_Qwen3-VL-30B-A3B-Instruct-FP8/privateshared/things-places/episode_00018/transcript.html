QUESTIONER: What is in the northwest?

The **northwest** is a region of the United States known for its rugged natural beauty, temperate rainforests, vibrant cities, and a rich blend of Indigenous, European, and modern cultures. It includes the following states:

- **Washington**
- **Oregon**
- **Idaho**
- **Montana**
- **Wyoming** (sometimes included, though often grouped with the Mountain West)
- **Alaska** (often considered part of the Northwest due to its geographic and cultural ties)
- **British Columbia** (in Canada, often culturally and geographically linked to the U.S. Northwest)

The region is sometimes referred to as the **Pacific Northwest (PNW)**—a term that emphasizes its proximity to the Pacific Ocean and its distinct climate and culture.

---

### Key Features of the Northwest:

#### 1. **Geography & Climate:**
- **Coastal Rainforests:** Lush, green forests along the Pacific coast, especially in Washington and Oregon (e.g., Olympic National Park, Redwood forests).
- **Mountains:** The Cascade Range runs through Washington and Oregon, with iconic peaks like **Mount Rainier**, **Mount Hood**, and **Mount St. Helens**.
- **Rivers & Waterfalls:** The Columbia River, Willamette River, and numerous waterfalls (e.g., Multnomah Falls).
- **Climate:** Mild, wet winters and cool, dry summers—especially in coastal areas. Inland areas (like eastern Washington and Idaho) are drier and more continental.

#### 2. **Major Cities:**
- **Seattle, Washington** – Tech hub (home to Microsoft, Amazon), music scene (grunge), and coffee culture.
- **Portland, Oregon** – Known for its sustainability, craft beer, and arts scene.
- **Spokane, Washington** – A cultural and educational center in eastern Washington.
- **Boise, Idaho** – A growing city with outdoor recreation and a vibrant downtown.
- **Missoula, Montana** – A college town surrounded by mountains and rivers.
- **Anchorage, Alaska** – A major city with access to glaciers, wildlife, and the Arctic.

#### 3. **Culture & Lifestyle:**
- **Outdoor Recreation:** Hiking, kayaking, skiing, fishing, and camping are central to life in the region.
- **Indigenous Peoples:** Home to many Native American tribes, including the Coast Salish, Nez Perce, and Tlingit, with deep cultural traditions.
- **Environmental Awareness:** Strong focus on sustainability, conservation, and protecting natural resources.
- **Arts & Music:** Known for its music scene (e.g., grunge, indie rock), visual arts, and literary culture.
- **Food & Drink:** Famous for Pacific Northwest cuisine—salmon, oysters, wild mushrooms, and craft beer.

#### 4. **Natural Wonders:**
- **National Parks & Monuments:**
- **Olympic National Park (WA)** – Rainforests, mountains, and coastline.
- **Mount Rainier National Park (WA)** – A snow-capped volcano and alpine wilderness.
- **Crater Lake National Park (OR)** – The deepest lake in the U.S., formed in a volcanic caldera.
- **Yellowstone National Park (WY)** – Though often associated with the Mountain West, it’s near the Northwest and shares cultural ties.
- **Glacier National Park (MT)** – Known as the "Crown of the Continent."

#### 5. **Economy & Innovation:**
- **Technology:** Seattle is a global tech hub, with companies like Microsoft, Amazon, and Boeing.
- **Agriculture:** Produces apples, cherries, wine (especially in Oregon’s Willamette Valley), and hops.
- **Renewable Energy:** A leader in hydroelectric power and wind energy.

- The Northwest is a place of **deep connection to nature**—where the land, water, and sky shape identity.

- The **Pacific Northwest** is often seen as a place of **peace, creativity, and environmental consciousness**.
